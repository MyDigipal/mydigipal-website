Two LinkedIn announcements in one week tell you where B2B ad money is going. On 28 October, LinkedIn reported that paid video grew 25% year over year and creator-led ads grew 63%. Six days earlier, it opened its connected TV ads to programmatic buyers through Google's Display & Video 360.

The direction is clear. Budgets are moving away from static company-page ads and towards people and video. The question for an ABM team is how far to follow, and in what order.

## What the numbers say, and what they leave out

The figures come from LinkedIn's highlights for Microsoft's first fiscal quarter of 2027. Alongside the ad numbers, LinkedIn says Feed content views rose more than 16% and time spent engaging with that content rose over 15%. Established creators posting daily grew 13%, about six times faster than the broader creator base.

These are growth rates, and they deserve a careful reading. LinkedIn does not say how much of its ad revenue video or creator ads represent. A format that grows 63% from a small base can still be a minor line in most media plans. The post also says nothing about cost per lead or pipeline.

What the numbers do show is supply and demand moving together. More creators post every day, members spend more time in the Feed, and advertisers are paying to sit inside that content rather than next to it. In practice, that tends to push prices up on the formats everyone wants, so early tests usually cost less than late ones.

## CTV on LinkedIn: what you can actually buy

The 22 October announcement is narrower than the headlines suggest. LinkedIn says advertisers can buy its CTV ads through Campaign Manager or through selected demand-side platforms, and Display & Video 360 now joins that list.

The targeting uses LinkedIn's first-party signals, including job title, industry and seniority. The inventory comes from premium streaming placements through Microsoft Monetize. Buyers in DV360 can use Google's Gemini-based tools for planning, optimisation and reporting.

LinkedIn claims its CTV ads are 2.6 times more effective at driving awareness among B2B audiences than other CTV offerings, and twice as effective when paired with other LinkedIn formats. The announcement does not explain the method, so treat those as vendor figures until your own brand lift study says otherwise.

The limit that matters most for our clients is geography. The DV360 option "is currently available to customers for US targeting". For a European software company selling into France or the UK, CTV on LinkedIn is a 2027 watch item for now.

## Four formats, four jobs

Each format answers a different question in an account-based plan. Mixing them up is how budgets get wasted.

| Format | Main job in ABM | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Thought Leader Ads | Put your executives and experts in front of named accounts | The author must approve each post, single image or video only |
| Creator-led ads | Borrow trust and reach in a market where nobody knows you | Less control over the message, audience fit to check |
| Brand video ads | Build engaged-viewer pools for retargeting | Needs a list large enough to deliver |
| CTV | Awareness on the biggest screen in the house | US targeting only, no click, measured by lift |

Thought Leader Ads let you sponsor posts from employees, industry experts, customers or creators, once the author approves the request. They run on awareness, engagement and video view objectives. We covered how to produce them at scale in [our Thought Leader Ads framework](/en/blog/linkedin-thought-leader-ai-roi).

## Where we would put a 2027 ABM budget

Here is the order we would follow for a B2B tech company with a few hundred to a few thousand target accounts.

**First, Thought Leader Ads from your own people.** Two or three executives who post something useful every week, sponsored to the account list. Production is cheap and the content is credible. It also reaches buyers in the Feed, where time spent is growing. In our view, this is where the largest share of a test budget should go.

**Second, short video for engagement and retargeting.** Thirty to sixty seconds, one idea, subtitles on. The goal is to build a pool of people at target accounts who watched most of a video, then follow up with offers aimed at that pool.

**Third, creators when you need reach you have not earned yet.** Entering a new country or launching a new category are good reasons. Pick creators by the overlap between their audience and your account list, and accept that you are buying their voice along with their reach.

**Last, CTV if your accounts are in the US and your list is large.** It is an awareness play. Plan it with a brand lift study from the start, because nobody will click a TV ad.

## The limits nobody puts in the press release

**Audience size.** A company list must match at least 300 LinkedIn members before it can run, and LinkedIn recommends lists of 1,000 companies or more. A tight list of 80 strategic accounts will struggle to feed video retargeting, and CTV makes little sense at that scale. For very small lists, [micro-ABM tactics](/en/blog/micro-abm-mid-market-scaling-tactics) usually beat media spend.

**Geography.** CTV through DV360 is US only. Creator supply also varies by market, and a creator who works in English may not move a French buyer.

**Measurement.** Video and CTV rarely produce last-click conversions. If your reporting only counts form fills, these formats will look like waste. Measure engagement by account and pipeline influence instead, or do not start.

## Our take

LinkedIn is building its ad products around a simple observation. Buyers respond to people more readily than to logos. Before you move any 2027 budget, check two numbers. How many of your target accounts sit in the US, and how many LinkedIn members does your account list match? Those two answers settle most of the plan, from whether CTV is even an option to how much video your list can feed.

If your LinkedIn plan still rests mostly on static single-image ads, start with [the basics of a LinkedIn strategy that converts](/en/blog/successful-linkedin-strategy), then test one executive voice for a quarter before moving more budget.
