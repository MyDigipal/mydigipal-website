Google has tightened what dealerships can claim about price. Advertised monthly payments and pricing claims must now mirror the actual transaction details a shopper finds on your website. Get it wrong and ads get disapproved. Get it wrong repeatedly and the account is at risk.

This is being read across the industry as a direct response to regulators pushing on hidden fees in car advertising. Whatever the motive, the practical effect is the same: the gap between your feed and your VDP is now a compliance problem, not a housekeeping annoyance.

## Where the mismatches actually come from

In the dealer accounts we audit, almost nobody is trying to mislead anyone. The mismatches are structural.

**Payment terms hard-coded in the feed.** Someone set a monthly payment field months ago, based on an APR and a term that were current then. The rate moved. The feed did not.

**Expired offers still live.** A seasonal promotion ended on the site but survives in the feed because the two systems are updated by different people on different schedules.

**Fees disclosed on the VDP but not reflected in the advertised figure.** Documentation fees, delivery, preparation. If the advertised payment excludes what the buyer will actually pay, that is the exact gap the rule targets.

**Sync latency.** A daily feed refresh means a price change on the site can be out of step with live ads for up to twenty-four hours. On a fast-moving used inventory, that window matters.

## The checklist we run

Six checks, in this order. Most dealers find a problem in the first three.

1. **Pull the feed and the VDP for twenty random vehicles.** Compare advertised price, monthly payment, term and APR line by line. Twenty is enough to know whether you have a systemic problem or an isolated one.
2. **Find every hard-coded payment field.** Anything not calculated from a live rate is a future mismatch. Either automate it or remove it from the ad.
3. **Check that fees are consistent.** If the VDP adds a documentation fee at the end, the advertised figure needs to account for it or the claim needs rewording.
4. **Shorten the sync window.** Daily is the minimum. If your inventory moves fast, push to more frequent updates on price fields specifically.
5. **Set up disapproval alerts.** Do not discover this through a drop in traffic three weeks later. Alerts on disapproved items belong in whatever channel your team actually reads.
6. **Document who owns the price field.** Most of these problems are ownership problems. If the website team and the feed team both assume the other maintains it, nobody does.

## Who owns the price field

Most of the fixes above are technical. This one is organisational, and it is the reason the problem comes back six months after you solve it.

In a typical group, the vehicle price exists in three places. The DMS holds the transaction price. The website displays a price built from the DMS plus whatever rules the web agency implemented. The feed carries a third version, often assembled by a feed management tool with its own transformation logic.

Three systems, three owners, and usually no agreement on which is authoritative. When the finance rate changes, whoever remembers updates their own copy.

The fix is a sentence, written down and agreed: the DMS is the source of truth for price, the site and the feed both derive from it, and nobody edits a price downstream. It sounds obvious. Almost no group we audit has actually said it out loud, which is why the drift keeps happening.

For multi-site groups, add a second sentence naming one person accountable across all locations. Distributed ownership of a price field means nobody owns it.

## What this costs if you ignore it

Worth being precise about the escalation, because "ads might get disapproved" sounds survivable.

A disapproved item stops serving. On a used inventory where a handful of vehicles drive most of the enquiries, losing those listings for a week is a measurable revenue hit, not an inconvenience.

Repeated violations put the account at risk. An account suspension during a plate-change month or an end-of-quarter push is the kind of event that a dealer principal remembers for years, and it takes days to resolve rather than hours.

And there is a slower cost. Google is responding to regulatory pressure here. Advertising rules that mirror consumer protection expectations tend to tighten rather than relax, so a feed that only just complies today will need work again. A feed built on a single source of truth will not.

## The upside nobody mentions

There is a version of this change that is pure cost. Audit the feed, fix the fields, move on.

We would argue the opposite. Price mismatch is one of the most reliable drop-off points on a vehicle detail page. A shopper clicks an ad showing one monthly payment, lands on a page showing another, and leaves. That lost session was already costing you money before Google made it a policy issue.

Dealers who fix the feed properly tend to see the effect on landing page engagement, not just on disapprovals. You are removing a moment of distrust at the exact point where the buyer was ready to enquire.

## Two other things that landed at the same time

Worth knowing while you are in the account. Vehicle ads in the US expanded to cover ATVs, RVs, utility task vehicles and non-motorised trailers such as travel trailers and campers. If you carry any of that inventory and were not advertising it, the surface is now open.

Performance Max also picked up campaign-level negative keywords, full channel reporting and Waze integration for store visits. Dealers now have two real routes for vehicle inventory, since Standard Shopping gives you tighter feed label logic, inventory segmentation and budget isolation. Which one fits depends on how much control you need over segmentation, and we go into that on our [dealership Google Ads page](/en/automotive/google-ads).

If your inventory feed is also powering [dynamic ads on Meta](/en/automotive/dynamic-ads), fix the source rather than each destination. One clean feed is cheaper to maintain than three patched ones. And if the tracking behind all of it is unclear, [that audit comes first](/en/services/tracking-reporting).
